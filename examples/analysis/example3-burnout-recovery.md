## Why it works

### 1. The hook is a transformation story with stakes
"How I rebuilt my brain after complete burnout **(when everyone said I'd never be the same)**". Two ingredients:
- **"How I…"** = proof-of-transformation, implies it worked, implies a method you can copy.
- **"when everyone said I'd never be the same"** = raises the stakes and adds an underdog/defiance hook. You want to know how she beat the odds.

### 2. First-person "I did X" is more believable than "you should do X"
Every slide is `N. I [action]`. It's a confession/diary, not instruction. People trust *"here's what actually worked for me"* far more than commands. It also removes all preachiness — she's not telling you what to do, just what she did.

### 3. Emotionally specific, permission-giving lines
`I gave myself permission to be completely useless for 3 months` / `no "getting back out there"` — this gives the reader *permission* to do the thing they secretly want (rest without guilt). Permission-granting content gets saved and sent to friends ("this is your sign").

### 4. Each slide = action + the deeper why
`I walked outside every day` + `fresh air became my medicine when my brain felt like static`. The metaphor ("brain felt like static", "holds onto everything like a hoarder") makes abstract feelings concrete and quotable. Quotable lines get shared.

### 5. The plug is a soft URL mid-list
`useventy.com was my go to for this` on slide 6 of 8 — again **middle-to-late, never slide 1, framed as "my go-to."** Same pattern as Examples 1 & 2. (Note: the underlying handwritten-journal image is a nice touch — the visual reinforces the "get it out of your head" message.)

### 6. Consistent warm/cozy visual world
Every image is warm-lit, homey, slightly moody — robe, window light, candles, journal, city night. The visual palette *is* the feeling of recovery. Cohesion across slides makes it feel like one intentional story, not random stock.

## The transferable formula

> **HOOK:** `How I [achieved hard transformation] (when [nobody believed / it felt impossible])`
> **BODY (×6-7):** `N. I [specific action]` → `[the deeper emotional why, with one vivid metaphor]`
> **PLUG:** middle-to-late slide, `[product] was my go-to for this`.
> **VOICE:** first-person confession, permission-granting, one quotable metaphor per slide.
> **IMAGES:** one cohesive warm/atmospheric world across all slides.

## The key contrast vs. Example 2
- **Example 2 = "you" (symptom checklist)** → triggers self-diagnosis, "do I have this?"
- **Example 3 = "I" (personal recovery)** → triggers aspiration + permission, "I want that, and it's allowed."

Both are top-tier save formats for the same niche. **We should have both in our rotation** — the "you" diagnostic and the "I" story. They hit different psychological triggers and can even be paired (post a symptom carousel, then a recovery carousel).
