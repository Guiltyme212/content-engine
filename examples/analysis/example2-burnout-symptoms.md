## Why it works

### 1. The hook attacks a blind spot ("you didn't realize")
"5 physical things **you didn't realize**…" — this is a curiosity gap AND a self-diagnosis trigger. The viewer immediately thinks *"wait, do I have these?"* and has to check every slide against their own body. That's not passive reading, it's active self-scanning. Unskippable.

### 2. Symptom format = personal relevance on every slide
Each slide is a **symptom + validation + micro-explanation**:
- Symptom: `your teeth feel sore for no reason`
- Validation: `not a cavity. not gum issues.` (rules out the boring explanation, confirms "this is you")
- Mechanism: `you've probably been clenching all day without noticing.`

The reader keeps going because they need to know if they "have" the next one. Fear/health checklists are one of the highest-save formats that exist.

### 3. Specificity sells the truth
`phone. keys. water bottle.` — the concrete, staccato list of dropped objects feels *observed*, not invented. Fake-general ("you drop things") is skippable; hyper-specific ("phone. keys. water bottle. random objects.") feels like it was written about you specifically.

### 4. Lowercase, therapist-adjacent, gentle voice
No caps, no exclamation, no shouting. "it's genuinely been life changing" with a `<3`. This is the **exact register the Kokoro tenant needs** — intimate, non-clinical, no toxic positivity. It reads like a friend who's been to therapy, not a brand.

### 5. The app plug is on slide 3 (the middle), disguised as personal habit
`i use the vent now app to process what i'm feeling` — dropped in the MIDDLE of the list, framed as *her therapist's recommendation + her own routine*, ending in `<3`. It's not "download this app." It's "here's what I personally do." Note: the same mechanic appears in Example 1 (slide 4). **Mid-carousel, personal-habit-framed plug is the pattern.**

### 6. Text-on-aesthetic-background format
Unlike Example 1 (real photos + white overlay text), this uses **soft blurred/gradient backgrounds** (dusk skies, muted color washes) with dark centered text. This is the *calmer, more "mental health"* visual language — and it's exactly the direction that matches Kokoro's Japanese-minimalism dusk aesthetic. Cover is a real lifestyle photo; the body slides go abstract/atmospheric so the words carry the weight.

### 7. The caption adds a 6th tip
`buying a mouth guard to sleep in… helps with #1` — the caption itself continues the value, seeding a comment-worthy detail and rewarding people who read down.

## The transferable formula

> **HOOK:** `[number] [physical/hidden] things you didn't realize were [symptoms of X]` *(soft parenthetical stakes)*
> **BODY (×5):** `N) [specific symptom]` → `[rule out the boring cause]` → `[relatable detail]` → `[why it happens]`
> **PLUG:** middle slide, framed as "my therapist / my routine / the app I use to process," end warm (`<3`).
> **VOICE:** lowercase, gentle, no toxic positivity.
> **IMAGES:** soft atmospheric/gradient backgrounds; real lifestyle photo only on the cover.

## Why this is the template for Kokoro
The voice ("i won't try to fix you, i'll just listen"), the register (lowercase, gentle), the symptom→validation→relief arc, and the atmospheric visuals map 1:1 onto Kokoro's brand. A Kokoro post could be *"5 signs your mind never actually gets to rest"* → symptom checklist → mid-plug: *"i vent to kokoro at night and it makes me a meditation from my own words."*
