# Example 4 — the fixed-slot machine (60 slideshows, one template)

**Source:** a Google Sheet of 60 finished Kokoro slideshows written by a freelancer for Dan
(2026-07). Not a scraped winner — an *operator's production system*. Its value is not the copy;
it's the proof that a rigid slot template plus a pain list can produce sixty postable carousels
without a single creative decision per post.

Read this next to `00-what-makes-these-convert.md`. Those three examples show what a winning
carousel looks like. This one shows how to make sixty of them on a Tuesday.

## The template

Every one of the 60 posts is the same seven slots, in the same order:

| Slot | Job | The rule, observed in all 60 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Hook | 6–13 words. First or second person. Never names the product. |
| S2 | Credibility | **Literally identical every time: "Trust me, I know."** |
| S3 | Pain, stated behaviourally | A *tell*, never a feeling: "you stop replying to people you genuinely care about." |
| S4 | Deepen, or kill the myth | "Rest isn't something you earn. Your brain needs it to function." |
| S5 | The fix **+ the plug** | Past-tense habit, then a parenthetical: `(I use the Kokoro app.)` |
| S6 | Mechanism / reframe | "Mental exhaustion can feel exactly like physical exhaustion." |
| S7 | Aphorism, no plug | 6–10 words, screenshot-able: "Burnout whispers long before it screams." |

**Caption:** one takeaway sentence + 🤍 + a soft first-person plug + exactly 4 hashtags, on a
single line. (That last part is not style — TikTok photo captions cannot contain line breaks.)

**Sound:** a pool of ten trending sounds cycled in order, one per post, repeating every ten.
Sound is a slot too, filled by rotation, not by taste.

## Two structural details worth stealing

1. **S2 is a constant, not a beat to write.** The same three words carry every post. It buys
   the swipe from slide 1 to slide 3 — the most expensive swipe in the carousel — and costs
   zero authoring effort. It only works because it is never asked to be clever.
2. **Listicles put the plug at item 3 of 5.** In the "5 signs…" variants, S3–S7 are numbered
   `1.`–`5.` and the product cameo *is* item 3 — dead centre, with two useful items after it.
   This independently confirms the mid-carousel cameo rule in §1 of the playbook: the product
   is never the conclusion.

## The ten hook shapes it rotates

Listicle ("5 signs you're X") · confession-reversal ("I thought X… but I was wrong") ·
withheld secret ("the X nobody warned me about") · hindsight ("I wish someone had told me
this before…") · anti-advice ("everyone tells you to rest… but nobody explains this") ·
diagnosis ("the real reason I was always tired") · borrowed authority ("a Harvard
psychologist explained why…") · trend framing ("people are beginning to realize…") ·
rating ("rating everything that helped me recover") · direct order ("if you're always tired,
read this").

## The pain list underneath

The 60 posts are 60 *pains*, not 60 ideas: burnout, emotional exhaustion, overthinking,
survival mode, brain fog, numbness, people-pleasing, perfectionism, productivity anxiety,
nervous system, doomscrolling, self-worth, boundaries, inner critic, morning anxiety, feeling
behind, racing mind at night, high-functioning anxiety, deferred happiness, self-awareness.

**This is the reusable insight and the reason the engine now has an audience layer.** The
template is cheap; the pain list is the asset. A generator with a good pain list and a fixed
skeleton beats a generator asked to be creative from a positioning statement.

## The visual layer (a gap in our engine)

The sheet also carries image-generation prompts, and they describe a different visual strategy
than ours: upload reference photos of **one real person**, then generate 6 variations per
reference — same person, same room, same lighting, different pose/angle/outfit — "as if taken
during the same photoshoot, a few minutes apart." Ultra-realistic editorial/lifestyle, natural
skin texture, no beauty filters, no AI artifacts, 9:16. Plus an account name and bio, i.e. a
consistent AI persona running the account.

Our engine generates **textless aesthetic backgrounds**. Theirs generates **a consistent
human**. Both reference winners in `00-what-makes-these-convert.md` have a real person on the
cover, so this is the same lesson arriving from a second direction. Not built; noted.

## Where it is weaker than it looks

All 60 posts plug Kokoro as generic journaling or "checking in with my emotions". None of them
use the actual mechanic — *you talk for two minutes and it makes a meditation out of your own
words*. That is fine for reach and weak for installs: the plug is interchangeable with any
mood-tracker, so a viewer has no reason to want *this* app. When the engine writes S5, the plug
must name the specific thing the product does, not the category it belongs to.

Second, the sheet is sentence-case throughout. The Kokoro tenant's voice is lowercase and
intimate. Voice is per-tenant data — the template is brand-agnostic, the casing is not.
