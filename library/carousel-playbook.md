# Carousel Playbook

This file is the **copy/knowledge source of truth** for the content engine. Production uses a short,
brand-agnostic runtime excerpt maintained in `server/hook-engine.js`; update that excerpt whenever the
transferable strategy here changes. Reference brands, product facts, and claims never enter the runtime excerpt.
This playbook can grow as analysis adds measured winners to the hook bank and format sections.

North star: **saves are the KPI, not likes.** All three reference winners run 15–48% save/like.
A carousel is a reference the viewer wants to keep — if it isn't save-worthy, it fails.

---

## 1. Format skeletons

### A. Content-first list or story with one soft tool cameo — DEFAULT
`hook debt → useful content × 2 → personal-tool cameo → useful content × 1-3 → payoff`
- Slide 1 never names the company, app, product, feature, CTA, or solution.
- The first two body slides must earn attention with concrete value before any product appears.
- The product appears once, around the middle, as a short first-person habit ("the tool I use"), never an ad.
- At least one useful editorial slide follows the cameo. The product is never the conclusion.

**Body shapes for A — pick ONE per carousel and repeat it on every body slide.** The viewer
learns the shape on slide 2; from then on swiping is free. Never mix shapes in one post. A1–A3
are verified winners described in the reference analysis later in this playbook; A4 is a
production template proven at volume rather than by one viral post:

- **A1 · Tip listicle — "the routine"** (proven: `examples/example1/`, 397K ❤️ / 145.8K 🔖)
  Slide shape: `N. [specific action] ([2-4 word benefit])` — action AND payoff on every slide.
  Universal across niches (fitness, finance, skincare, plants, food); search-seedable — answer
  a query people actually type. Cameo = the tool that solves one mid-list tip
  ("I track mine in ______").
- **A2 · Symptom / signs checklist — "the mirror"** (proven: `examples/example2/`, 105K ❤️ / 16.2K 🔖)
  Slide shape: `N) [specific symptom]` → `[rule out the boring cause]` → `[felt description]`
  → `[why it happens]`. "You"-voice self-diagnosis: the reader checks every slide against
  themselves, which makes it unskippable. Native for problem-aware emotional niches (mental
  health, sleep, burnout). Cameo = "my therapist has me… i use ______ to process" inside a
  mid symptom slide.
- **A3 · Personal story / transformation — "the diary"** (proven: `examples/example3/`, ~48% save/like)
  Slide shape: `N. I [specific action]` → `[deeper emotional why + one vivid metaphor]`
  ("my brain felt like static"). "I"-voice confession — permission-giving, never preachy:
  "here's what I did" beats "here's what you should do". Cameo = "______ was my go-to for
  this", mid-to-late. Pairs naturally with A2 for the same audience: first the "you"
  checklist, then the "I" recovery story.

- **A4 · Fixed-slot pain machine — "the assembly line"** (proven at volume:
  `examples/analysis/example4-fixed-slot-machine.md`, 60 posts from one template)
  Seven fixed slots, same order every time:
  `hook → constant credibility line → pain stated as a behaviour → deepen or kill the myth →
  the fix + the cameo → mechanism/reframe → aphorism`.
  Slot 2 is a **constant** ("trust me, i know." in that operator's voice) — it buys the
  slide-1→3 swipe, the most expensive one in the carousel, and costs nothing to author. Slot 3
  must be a *tell*, not a feeling ("you stop replying to people you care about", not
  "you feel disconnected"). Slot 5 is the only product moment; slot 7 carries no plug and is
  written to be screenshotted. In the numbered variant, slots 3–7 are items `1.`–`5.` and the
  cameo is item **3** — dead centre, two useful items after it.
  Caption formula: one takeaway + a soft first-person plug + exactly 4 hashtags, single line
  (TikTok photo captions cannot contain line breaks). Use this shape when generating a *batch*
  aimed at a pain list: it is the cheapest reliable way to turn N pains into N postable
  carousels. Its known failure mode: the cameo drifts into naming the category instead of the
  product's actual mechanic — see §3.

### B. Before/after proof
`hook (bold claim) → proof pair × 3-4 → opinionated payoff (comparison list)`
- Needs REAL imagery for the proof (satellite shots, screenshots, photos). Never AI-fake factual proof.

### C. Psychology facts
`hook (authority or curiosity) → fact × 4-5 → meta/share-trigger slide → emotional payoff + save CTA`
- Every fact must be defensible (real studies) — comment-section fact-checkers are part of distribution.

## 2. Hook bank (patterns, fill the blank)

**Cover anatomy — the cover decides ~90% of performance.** Every winning cover has three
parts, readable in < 2 seconds: (1) a concrete desired outcome or curiosity gap, (2) stakes /
reason-to-care-now — often a soft parenthetical ("this summer", "and you need to slow down",
"when everyone said I'd never be the same") — and (3) an **open loop that cannot close on
slide 1** (a colon, a count, a "how i"). The first three patterns below are the winners' exact
formulas, mapping to body shapes A1/A2/A3 in §1.

Two more cover laws (2026-07-24, learned the hard way — every reference winner does both):
- **A real human on the cover.** All three winning covers are a real person in a candid
  photo — the human is the scroll-stopper; an empty aesthetic room is invisible on the FYP.
  Two proven moves: emotional CONTRAST (happy sunny photo, heavy topic — ex2) or EMBODIMENT
  (the person visibly IS the promised outcome — ex1). Pull from the tenant's real photo
  library; never AI-generate a fake "creator" face.
- **Blunt, visceral, dramatic wording.** "shrink your waist", "serious burnout", "rebuilt my
  brain" — big concrete nouns and physical stakes. Soft poetic phrasing ("your mind never
  gets to rest") whispers; winners shout. Hook text renders LARGE and heavy, dominating its
  third of the frame.

Hook concreteness laws (2026-07-24, distilled from the taste-approved batch in §6):
- **Name where the problem lives** — a body part, a clock time, a scene the reader has stood
  in — never the abstract concept for it ("crying in the car after work" > "emotional
  exhaustion"; "your paycheck is gone by the 12th" > "bad budgeting").
- **Severity + real timeframes** raise stakes and credibility: "for years", "after 6 years
  of", "every single night", "a year of".
- **The parenthetical does ONE precise job:** mind-read ("yes, that one"), objection-kill
  ("without journaling for an hour"), twist that makes the answer unguessable ("it wasn't
  about sleep"), or numbered tease ("#3 is why you're always tired").
- **Adjacent territory is fair game.** The hook can live in any true moment of the audience's
  life, not only the brand's subject — the mid-carousel cameo is the only bridge to the
  product. The story turns into the ad later; the hook never does.

- "how to [desired outcome] [real constraint or timeframe]:" (search intent + open list)
- "N [concrete things] you didn't realize were [hidden tension]" (blind spot + self-scan)
- "how i [specific change] after [specific low point]" + an unresolved stake (first-person story)
- "for the ones who ______ all day" (identity + exhaustion)
- "POV: you ______ and nobody noticed" (identity + being seen)
- "______ isn't weakness. it's ______." (reframe)
- "you don't need ______. you need ______." (permission)
- "signs you're ______ (not ______)" (self-diagnosis listicle)
- "doctors in ______ prescribe ______ instead of ______" (authority + curiosity)
- "the magic number is ______" (specificity)
- "things nobody tells you about ______"
- "your brain is ______ years old. it still thinks ______." (evolutionary reframe)
- Numbers and hyper-specific first-person phrasing beat generic labels ("i would be unstoppable" > "glow up guide").
- A hook owes the reader a specific answer. If one slide fully answers it, it is a tagline, not a carousel hook.
- Product-free test: delete the company profile. The hook should still work as useful editorial content.

## 3. Slide craft rules

- Slide 1 decides everything: if it doesn't stop the scroll, nothing else matters.
- Aim for ≤ 24 words per slide; 30 is the hard ceiling. One idea per slide. Completion
  (swipe-to-end) is the ranking signal — no filler slides.
- 5–8 slides. Drop-off rises sharply after slide 7.
- **Double payout:** every body slide gives a WHAT (action/symptom) AND a WHY (benefit/reason/
  mechanism). "No sugar after 6pm" fails; "No sugar after 6pm (lighter stomach)" ships. The
  parenthetical/second line is where the save value lives.
- **Specificity = credibility:** real numbers and concrete nouns ("12500 steps", "phone. keys.
  water bottle.", "3 months"). Vague quantities ("a lot", "regularly") read as fake and get
  skipped. Specifics must stay truthful — see the no-invention rule below.
- **Plug hard rules:** exactly ONE product cameo per carousel, on a middle-to-late slide
  (3/6 · 4/7 · 6/8) — never slide 1, never the last slide. First-person habit framing
  ("the tool i use", "my therapist has me…", "was my go-to for this"), warm close if the brand
  voice allows; no CTA verbs ("download", "sign up", "tap the link"). The cameo must solve the
  exact item on its own slide — it rides the trust built by the slides before it.
- Include one **share/save trigger**: a meta slide ("yes, including this one"), or "save this for ______".
- Soft CTA only. Let the content do the selling.
- Never invent medical mechanisms, unsafe advice, proof, or facts. A surprising detail is useful only when it is
  truthful and defensible; conversation tension is not permission for fake comment bait.
- Caption: one relatable line + a question (drives comments) + 4-6 niche hashtags. No line breaks on TikTok.
  Referencing a numbered item ("#1 helps me most <3") triggers re-swipes.
- Post 6–10pm local time of target market; attach trending calm audio on TikTok.

### Pre-flight: the save-worthiness test (run on every draft before human review)
1. **Cover test** — does slide 1 force a swipe (open loop unresolved)? If a screenshot of
   slide 1 alone satisfies, fail → rewrite the hook.
2. **Skeleton test** — same body shape on every slide? Mixed shapes → unify.
3. **Double-payout test** — every slide has both a what and a why? Bare instruction → add the why.
4. **Specificity test** — real numbers/concrete nouns, nothing vague, nothing invented?
5. **Plug test** — exactly one cameo, middle slide, personal-habit framing, zero CTA verbs?
Any failure → fix and re-run. All five pass → ship to human review (Dan still judges visuals).

## 4. Visual series rules

- One post = one style recipe (same light, palette, mood in every prompt) so the carousel reads as designed.
- **Architecture (locked 2026-07-20): real-font OVERLAY on textless backgrounds.** Generate a
  clean, textless background per slide (gpt-image-2, 1024x1536 portrait), then overlay real
  TikTok Sans text (TikTok's own editor font; Montserrat fallback) — white fill + thin black
  outline (~0.13em stroke, painted under the fill), TikTok-native look, draggable position.
  Never bake letters into the image. Demand exact spelling; a human reviews every slide.
- Bind the visual world to the content's emotional register, then hold it across all slides:
  - how-to/practical → authentic casual phone photos (mirror selfie, POV hands, lifestyle
    objects) — recipe A below.
  - emotional/calm → text on atmospheric backgrounds (dusk skies, candles, warm low light);
    the palette may shift across the set (gold → lavender → last-light red, per ex2) —
    recipe B. The cover is still a REAL person photo (see §2 cover laws) — only the body
    slides go atmospheric. Story/how-to sets keep human presence on body slides too: POV
    camera-roll shots (hands, knees, feet, held objects — no face needed).
- JPEG/WEBP only for TikTok photo posts (no PNG).
- No fake UI screenshots, no watermarks, no real-brand imitations.

## 5. Brand brief: Kokoro (kokoromind.com)

- App: Kokoro (心) — "a meditation made from your own words". "Vent your mind. Change your life."
- What it does: you speak what's on your mind → kokoro listens → makes a personalized 3–7 min meditation
  in a voice that fits the mood (gen-z raw / spiritual / bedtime). Private: no tracking, sharing, scoring.
- Audience: people (especially women / "quiet humans") wrecked after a hard day, who want to vent to
  something that feels like a supportive friend — not advice, not therapy-speak, not toxic positivity.
- Voice: lowercase, intimate, honest, slightly irreverent. Core promise: "i won't try to fix you.
  i'll just listen — and make you something."
- Aesthetic: Japanese minimalism — sakura, lanterns, dusk, rain on windows, warm rooms. Kanji accents (心).
- Do: validate ("that really was a lot"), permission-giving, nervous-system language, privacy point.
- Don't: "fix yourself", productivity framing, clinical terms, hard sells, emoji spam.
- CTA phrasing: "i vent to kokoro. it listens — then makes a meditation from my own words." + free on iOS.

### 2026-07-20 · Reference analysis: two viral app-plug carousels (added by Dan + Claude)

**Example 1 — `examples/example1/` · "how to shrink your waist this summer:" (@liyah, fitness)**
397.1K likes · 145.8K saves · 10.7K shares · 474 comments · 7 slides.
- Every slide is an AUTHENTIC casual phone photo (mirror selfie, Stanley cup, treadmill POV,
  gym mirror, bed POV). Zero designed graphics — reads as a friend's camera roll, not an ad.
- Text: TikTok-native bold white sans with black outline, short, centered-low.
- Tip formula: `N. claim (benefit)` — "1. No sugar after 6pm (lighter stomach)".
- **App plug is MID-carousel** (tip 3 of 6, slide 4/7): "(I track mine in HealthMeter)" and the
  photo itself is the app open on a phone. Not the last slide.
- One odd, disputed tip drove searches and comments. That explains the engagement, but it is not a transferable
  content rule: never publish an unsafe or false instruction to manufacture comment bait.

**Example 2 — `examples/example2/` · "5 physical things you didn't realize were symptoms of
serious burnout" (@jade, wellness — a direct Kokoro-format neighbor)**
105.4K likes · 16.2K saves · 2.5K shares · 6 slides.
- Hook over a HAPPY real photo (girl on a boat) — emotional contrast with the heavy topic.
- Slides 2–6: text-only on real dusk-sky photos; the sky palette SHIFTS across the set
  (warm gold → orange → lavender → blue → purple → red last-light). Near-black lowercase
  sans, centered, blank-line gaps between short paragraphs.
- Slide structure: `N) symptom` → felt description → mechanism ("chronic stress actually
  reduces fine motor coordination because your brain is overloaded"). Validation + authority.
- **App plug MID-carousel** (tip 3, slide 4/6), framed through authority + affection:
  "my therapist has me consistently check in… i use the vent now app to process what i'm
  feeling so it doesn't silently pile up <3 it's genuinely been life changing."
- Caption drives swipe-back: "buying a mouth guard to sleep in and exercise in helps with #1 <3"
  — references a numbered tip, so readers re-open the carousel.

**Example 3 — `examples/example3/` · "How I rebuilt my brain after complete burnout" (recovery story)**
6.7K likes · 3.2K saves · 312 shares · 73 comments · 8 slides.
- The hook combines a first-person transformation story with an unresolved social stake:
  `(when everyone said I'd never be the same)`. The reader must swipe to learn what changed.
- Slides 2–8 use a numbered first-person action + honest reason grammar. The actions are concrete and visually
  ordinary: time off, repeated meals, a short walk, writing/talking, rest, and boundaries.
- The product appears once on slide 6 as the creator's personal tool, after four organic steps. Two useful steps
  follow it, so the carousel does not end on an advertisement.
- Casual camera-roll photos make the story feel lived rather than staged. Each image literally matches the action.
- Transfer the narrative grammar and placement only. Claims about burnout, recovery, the brain, food, sleep, or
  health belong to that creator and must not be copied or treated as evidence without verification.

**Rule updates adopted from this analysis:**
1. **Mid-carousel plug beats last-slide plug.** Weave the product once into a middle item as a
   personal tool habit. Keep useful content after it; the last slide remains an editorial payoff.
2. Two proven visual recipes: (A) authentic casual photos + white outlined text (needs real
   user photos); (B) text-on-dusk-sky gradient set with shifting palette (fully generatable,
   native fit for Kokoro).
3. Include a surprising but truthful and defensible detail or conversation tension. Never invent unsafe advice,
   medical mechanisms, or false facts for comment bait.
4. Caption must reference a numbered tip ("#1 helps me most <3") to trigger re-swipes.
5. Slide dots (position indicator) are part of the native format — preview UI must show them.
6. Last slide = meta/emotional closer ("you're reading this instead of sleeping. that's the
   sign.") — doubles as save trigger.

### 2026-07-30 · The audience layer: pains become the input, not an afterthought

Source: 60 finished Kokoro slideshows a freelancer wrote for Dan in one Google Sheet, torn down
in `examples/analysis/example4-fixed-slot-machine.md`. The finding that changed the engine: the
sheet is not 60 ideas, it is **one rigid seven-slot template run over a list of 20 pains**. The
template is cheap and repeatable; the pain list is the asset. Folded in as body shape **A4** in
§1.

What this changed in the product (built the same day):

- The company profile now carries an **audience layer** alongside positioning: 6–10 *pains*
  (label + the behavioural tell + the emotional cost), *beliefs to break*, the audience's own
  *words*, and a *product bridge* (the product restated as one habit, plus the plug line and the
  claims to never make). Extracted from the tenant's own site in the same pass as the brand
  profile, then **editable by the operator** in the Studio — pains can be deleted, added, or
  **pinned** to force them into the next batch.
- `editorialTopic()` now aims a batch at those pains instead of asking the model to rediscover
  the audience every run; `briefBlock()` ships the whole layer into every prompt as data.
- Every text/craft call runs on a **current Claude model**. No legacy models anywhere in the
  copy layer (the brand/audience extraction used to fall back to `gpt-4o`): cheap old models
  write generic, off-voice hooks and miss these rules, and this is the layer that decides what
  a post is about. OpenAI remains for image generation only.

Two craft rules the sheet earned, both from its weaknesses:

1. **The cameo must name the mechanic, not the category.** All 60 posts plug Kokoro as generic
   journaling / "checking in with my emotions" — interchangeable with any mood tracker, so the
   viewer has no reason to want *this* app. Slot 5 has to say the specific thing the product
   does ("i said one sentence out loud and it made me a 4-minute meditation out of it"), not the
   category it belongs to.
2. **A constant credibility slide is free swipe-buying.** One fixed line in slot 2, identical
   across every post in the series, carries the slide-1→3 swipe — the most expensive one in the
   carousel — precisely because it is never asked to be clever. Per-tenant data (its wording is
   voice), not a global default.
